# GLOSSARY.md format

`GLOSSARY.md` is the canonical language of the teaching workspace. Lessons, exercises, and learning records use its terms. Building it is part of the learning. A concept the user can compress into a tight definition is a concept the user understands.

Create it only after the user shows they understand a term. Never on the first run.

## Structure

```md
# {Topic} glossary

{One or two sentences on the topic this glossary covers.}

## Terms

**Hypertrophy**:
Muscle growth driven by mechanical tension and metabolic stress over repeated training sessions.
_Avoid_: bulking, getting big

**Progressive overload**:
Raising the demand on a muscle over time through load, volume, or intensity.
_Avoid_: pushing harder, levelling up

**RPE (rate of perceived exertion)**:
A 1 to 10 rating of how hard a set felt. 10 is failure. 8 means two reps left.
_Avoid_: effort score, intensity rating
```

## Rules

- **Add a term only when the user understands it.** The glossary records compressed knowledge. It is not a dictionary the user reads to learn. Wait until the user can use a new concept correctly.
- **Be opinionated.** When several words name one concept, pick the best and list the rest under `_Avoid_`. This is how language compresses.
- **Keep definitions tight.** One or two sentences. Say what the term is, not what it does or how to do it.
- **Use the glossary's own terms inside definitions.** Once a term is in, prefer it everywhere. That makes later terms easier to grasp.
- **Group under subheadings** when clusters emerge, such as `## Anatomy` and `## Programming`. A flat list is fine when the terms cohere.
- **Flag ambiguity.** When the wider field uses a term loosely, state the workspace's reading. "Here, 'set' always means a working set. Warm-ups are tracked separately."
- **Revise as understanding deepens.** A week-one definition can be wrong by week six. Update it in place.
